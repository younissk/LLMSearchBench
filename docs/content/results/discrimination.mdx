# Search-result discrimination results

A first run of the [second task](/docs/tasks/search-result-discrimination/): a
question, a fixed candidate set, and the question of which results actually
bear on it.

Switch between the slices to read it by category and by noise tier. The
interesting column is not the ranking one.

<DiscriminationResults />

## What this first run says

**Ranking is nearly saturated.** nDCG@10 sits between 93% and 99%, and on the
`wikipedia` category the best model reaches 95.6% precision and 96.7% recall.
When the answer is somewhere in the pile, these models find it. The task as
built barely separates them there.

**The separation is entirely in `no_answer`.** Those are items where a human
assessor read every candidate against that exact question and graded all of
them 0. Qwen cited noise on 7 of 15 of them; Nemotron on 8 of 15. Asked how
often button quail lay eggs — a question none of its candidates answer —
Nemotron cited one anyway and reported "about 90–120 eggs per year".

That is the failure this category exists to catch, and both models do it about
half the time.

## What is missing

- **Groundedness** is unscored: no judge has been run. The column is absent
  rather than zero.
- **The full 331 items.** This is a 45-item sample per model.
- **Free endpoints.** `qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free` returned nothing at all — its
  upstream pool is exhausted — so the Qwen row is the paid endpoint for the
  same weights. Gemma's row rests on far fewer items than the others and should
  not be compared with them.

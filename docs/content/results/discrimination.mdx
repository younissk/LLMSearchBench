# Search-result discrimination results

A question, a pile of search results, and one thing scored: **was the answer
right?**

Two ways to be right, and they are the whole task:

- the results contain the answer, and the model gave it;
- the results contain no answer, and the model said so instead of inventing one.

<DiscriminationResults />

## What this first run says

**Both models answer when they should not.** On items where a human assessor
read every result against that question and rejected all of them, Qwen answered
anyway 11 times out of 30 and Nemotron 14 out of 30. Asked *is caffeine a
narcotic* — with nothing in the results addressing it — Nemotron replied "No".
A reasonable answer, and not one that came from the evidence, which is the
failure this category exists to catch.

**Neither model makes the opposite mistake.** Zero wrong refusals, both models.
When the answer is there, they use it; they never say "not in the results" about
results that hold the answer. The error is entirely one-sided.

**Noise costs accuracy.** Qwen: 85% on five results, 65% on ten, 70% on twenty.
Nemotron: 75%, 65%, 55%. Thin evidence at 20 items per tier, but the direction
is what the tiers were built to show.

## What this does not measure

Whether the model ranked the results, which ones it would have cited, and
whether its wording matched the evidence. Those are separate abilities. Putting
them in the same run meant reporting four things at once and being able to say
nothing clean about any of them, so they were taken out.

## What is missing

- **The `web` category.** TREC graded which passages were relevant, never what
  the answer was, so those items carry no gold answer and cannot be scored this
  way. Their queries still serve as the `no_answer` items, where the right
  answer is that the results do not have one. Scorable set: 160 of the 331.
- **The full set.** This is a 60-item sample per model.
- **A third model.** `qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free` and `google/gemma-4-31b-it:free`
  could not get through their rate limits, so the Qwen row is the paid endpoint
  for the same weights.

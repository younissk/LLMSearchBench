# Search-result discrimination results

A question, a pile of search results, and one thing scored: **was the answer
right?**

Two ways to be right, and they are the whole task:

- the results contain the answer, and the model gave it;
- the results contain no answer, and the model said so instead of inventing one.

<DiscriminationResults />

## What this run says

`qwen/qwen3.8-27b` on all 199 scorable items: **72.4% correct**.

| Category | Items | Correct | |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| wikipedia | 120 | 92 | 76.7% |
| web | 39 | 26 | 66.7% |
| no_answer | 40 | 26 | 65.0% |

**It answers when it should not.** On the 40 items where a human read every
result against the question and rejected all of them, it produced an answer 14
times. Those answers are often true — asked *is caffeine a narcotic*, "No" is
correct — but they did not come from the evidence, which is the failure this
category exists to catch.

**The opposite error is rarer but real.** Seven times it said the results had no
answer when they did.

**Noise does not degrade it cleanly.** 74.6% on five results, 65.2% on ten,
77.3% on twenty. The dip in the middle is not a curve, and with ~66 items a
tier it is not yet evidence of anything.

## A label problem this run exposed

The first scoring of the web category read 43.1%, and it was my labelling, not
the model. Answers written to a six-word cap came out telegraphic — `one slice
cheese vs two`, `RN is license, BSN is degree` — and no model phrases things
that way, so correct answers were being marked wrong.

Gold answers must now be **a span copied out of the passage**, checked
automatically. That dropped 11 of 24 annotated queries, including every
comparison and cost-range one. The remaining 13 are the sort that have a single
right answer: *tracheids are part of _____* → `xylem`, *when was the salvation
army founded* → `1865`.

Web accuracy after the fix: 66.7%. The earlier 43.1% measured my labels.

## What this does not measure

Whether the model ranked the results, which ones it would have cited, and
whether its wording matched the evidence. Those are separate abilities. Putting
them in the same run meant reporting four things at once and being able to say
nothing clean about any of them, so they were taken out.

## What is missing

- **The `web` category.** TREC graded which passages were relevant, never what
  the answer was, so those items carry no gold answer and cannot be scored this
  way. Their queries still serve as the `no_answer` items, where the right
  answer is that the results do not have one. Scorable set: 160 of the 331.
- **The full set.** This is a 60-item sample per model.
- **A third model.** `qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free` and `google/gemma-4-31b-it:free`
  could not get through their rate limits, so the Qwen row is the paid endpoint
  for the same weights.

# What gets measured

Four things, reported separately. A single "accuracy" here would let a model
hide one failure behind another.

## The output the model has to produce

```json
{
  "ranking": [3, 4, 1, 5, 2],
  "relevant": [3, 4],
  "answer": "Notre Dame Fighting Irish football"
}
```

- `ranking` — every candidate id, best evidence first.
- `relevant` — the ones it would actually cite. Empty means *the results do not
  answer this*.
- `answer` — the answer those candidates support, or an explicit refusal.

Asking for the evidence is not decoration. A model that already knows who
replaced Tony Roberts can answer without reading a single candidate, and a
benchmark that only checks the answer would call that a success.

## 1. Ranking quality — nDCG@10

The standard information-retrieval measure, and the primary score. Discounted
cumulative gain at rank $k$, using the graded labels:

$$
\mathrm{DCG}@k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{2^{g_i} - 1}{\log_2(i + 1)}
$$

where $g_i$ is the grade of the candidate the model put at position $i$.
Normalising against the best possible ordering gives

$$
\mathrm{nDCG}@k = \frac{\mathrm{DCG}@k}{\mathrm{IDCG}@k}
$$

so 1.0 means the model ordered the candidates as well as the labels allow, and
0 means it put nothing useful in the top $k$.

Two properties matter here. The $2^{g}-1$ numerator makes a grade-3 passage
worth much more than a grade-1 one, and the $\log_2(i+1)$ denominator means
rank 1 counts for more than rank 10. Both are what you want when the question
is "did the evidence come first".

`nDCG@10` on the five-candidate tier is `nDCG@5` in effect — there is no
eleventh candidate to get wrong.

## 2. Evidence selection — precision, recall, F1

The `relevant` list, treated as a set against the candidates graded 2 or 3:

$$
P = \frac{|\text{chosen} \cap \text{supporting}|}{|\text{chosen}|},
\qquad
R = \frac{|\text{chosen} \cap \text{supporting}|}{|\text{supporting}|},
\qquad
F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P + R}
$$

Recall and precision fail in different directions and are reported apart.
Naming every candidate gives perfect recall and terrible precision; naming one
of two supporting paragraphs on a `wikipedia` item gives the reverse. The
false-positive rate on grade-0 candidates is reported too, because picking up
noise is the specific failure this task is about.

## 3. Abstention — on the `no_answer` category

For the 40 items where a human read every candidate and rejected all of them:

- **abstention rate** — how often the model says the results do not answer it.
- **fabrication rate** — how often it produces an answer anyway.

An answer here is wrong even when it is factually true, because it did not come
from the evidence. That is the point of the category.

The mirror error is measured on the other two categories: refusing to answer
when a graded-3 passage is sitting in the candidate set.

## 4. Answer quality — `wikipedia` only

- **correctness** — does the answer match a gold answer, compared leniently
  (accent- and punctuation-insensitive containment, as in the first task).
- **groundedness** — do the claims in the answer appear in the candidates the
  model cited? An answer that is right but rests on a candidate graded 0 is
  right for the wrong reason, and worth seeing.

The `web` category has no gold answer — TREC judged relevance, not answers — so
correctness is not scored there. Pretending otherwise would mean inventing
answers and calling them ground truth.

## Reading it by noise tier

Every score is also reported per tier, because the interesting number is not
the average but the slope:

| Tier | Candidates | Relevant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| easy | 5 | 1 |
| medium | 10 | 2 |
| hard | 20 | 4 |

Each `web` question appears at all three tiers with the same relevant
passages, so the comparison is within a question. A model that holds nDCG
steady from 5 to 20 candidates is doing something different from one that
collapses, and an average over the three would report them as the same.

## What is deliberately not scored

- **Fluency, length, style.** Not measured, not penalised.
- **Whether the model would have searched.** That is [task
  one](../tool-use-correctness/).
- **What it would have searched for.** That is [search
  quality](../search-quality), which does not exist yet.

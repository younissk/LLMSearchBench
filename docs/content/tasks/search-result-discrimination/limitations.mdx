# What this task cannot tell you

## The labels are two different kinds of thing

The `web` and `no_answer` grades are relevance judgements: a person read that
passage against that query and graded it. The `wikipedia` grades are derived
from HotpotQA's supporting-fact annotations — the dataset says which paragraphs
its supporting sentences came from, and this build turns that into grade 3
against grade 0.

Derived labels have no middle. A HotpotQA distractor that is genuinely on topic
and nearly answers the question is graded 0, the same as one about a different
continent. Every item records `label_source`, and the two kinds are never
averaged into one number, but the asymmetry does not go away by being labelled.

## Assessors are not infallible

TREC's judgements are the best available, not the truth. One example from the
set: for the query *anthropological definition of environment*, a passage about
**forensic** anthropology is graded 3. Arguably generous. It stays as graded,
because quietly re-judging someone else's assessments would make the labels
ours and undocumented.

## 57 questions is not many

The `web` category has 171 items but only **57 distinct questions** — each is
built at three noise tiers. The tiers are the point, and they support a
within-question comparison that 171 unrelated questions could not. But three
items from one question are not three independent observations, and any
confidence interval has to be computed over questions, not items.

TREC 2019 and 2020 judged 97 queries between them. That is the ceiling of this
source; a wider `web` category needs another year of the track or another
judged collection.

## The no-answer category is all from one year

The 40 reserved queries are the last 40 in build order, which are all from the
2020 track. Nothing about 2020 makes a query special, but it does mean the
category is not a random sample of the two years.

## Noise stops at twenty

The hard tier shows 20 candidates. Real search shows more, and the interesting
collapse may start somewhere past this ceiling. Twenty was chosen so the whole
candidate set fits in a modest context window without truncation becoming the
thing being measured — but it does mean the fall-off curve has three points on
it, not a continuum.

## Categories that are missing

The design admits any source of candidates. Three exist. These do not:

- **Conflicting results** — two candidates that disagree about a date, where
  the model has to resolve rather than copy. This needs authored contradictions
  or a counterfactual source; nothing here supplies them.
- **Outdated results** — a passage that was correct five years ago. Requires a
  time-stamped corpus.
- **Reasoning-heavy relevance** — where finding the relevant document needs a
  reasoning step rather than a semantic match. BRIGHT is the obvious source,
  but it publishes gold ids without negatives, so candidate sets would have to
  be built with a retriever, and the moment a retriever enters the pipeline
  the benchmark stops being retriever-agnostic.
- **Duplicate results** — the same fact three times, to see whether repetition
  reads as confirmation.

## The answer side is thin

Answer correctness is scored on 120 items — the `wikipedia` category — because
that is the only category whose source carries answers. The `web` category has
3.9 times as many candidates and no gold answer at all. Anything said about
answer quality here rests on the smaller half of the set.

## Nothing has been run

There is no harness and no leaderboard yet. The numbers on the [metrics
page](./metrics) describe what would be computed, not what was. Until a run
exists, none of this is evidence about any model.

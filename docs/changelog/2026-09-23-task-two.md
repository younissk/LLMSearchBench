A second task ships as a dataset: **search-result discrimination** — a
question, a fixed pile of search results, and one thing scored, whether the
answer was right.

<!-- truncate -->

## What is new

- **331 items, 199 scorable**, from TREC Deep Learning 2019/2020 judgements and
  HotpotQA's distractor split. Noise dominates every item by construction: one
  relevant result in five, two in ten, four in twenty.
- **A `no_answer` category** where a human read every result against the
  question and rejected all of them. The right answer is that the results do
  not have one. Most benchmarks leave this out, and leaving it out teaches a
  model that the answer is always somewhere in the pile.
- **Answers for the web queries**, written by Claude from passages a human
  already graded relevant, each one a verbatim span of its passage and checked
  as such.
- **A first run:** `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` at 72.4%, answering 14 times out of 40
  when the results had no answer to give.

## What the first task taught, applied here

Three scoring rules came out of running 36 models through tool-use correctness,
and all three are now invariants:

- a failed or silent reply is a missing measurement, not a wrong answer;
- a measure that does not apply to an item is blank, not zero;
- a label written by a model has to be checkable against the document it came
  from, or it is not a label.

The second one was found by this task's own first run: `no_answer` items were
scoring a free 1.0 on ranking and a 0.0 on precision, two errors that hid each
other until the numbers were read side by side.

## What is not here

No judge. Groundedness — whether an answer's claims appear in the results it
was given — has a design and no implementation, and the column is absent rather
than zero. Ranking and evidence-selection metrics were built and then removed:
they measured four things at once and separated nobody.
